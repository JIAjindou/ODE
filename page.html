Actions should not jump either

A discrete policy predicts each chunk of actions on its own, so the motion jumps where chunks meet. ODE policy learns how the action changes and integrates it over the robot's physical time, so each action follows from the previous one.

Method

ODE policy replaces only the action head. The encoder, the data, and the training schedule stay the same.

1Learn a velocity, not an action

$$\dot{\mathbf a}(t)=\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t),\,\mathbf s_t,\,\mathbf z_t\big)$$

The images and the instruction are encoded into an embedding $\mathbf z_t$, as in any policy. The network $\mathbf f_\theta$ then outputs how the action should change, given the action $\mathbf a(t)$ the robot is currently commanded to reach and its proprioception $\mathbf s_t$. Time $t$ is the physical time of the robot, counted in control steps.

Because the field sees the current action, each step depends on where the motion has arrived. A discrete policy outputs a whole chunk at once and cannot react to its own intermediate actions.

2Integrate chunk by chunk

$$\hat{\mathbf a}_{t+k+1}=\hat{\mathbf a}_{t+k}+\int_{t+k}^{t+k+1}\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t'),\,\mathbf s_t,\,\mathbf z_t\big)\,\mathrm dt',\quad k=0,\dots,H-1$$

Every $H=8$ steps, the policy reads the observation once and holds $\mathbf z_t$ and $\mathbf s_t$ fixed. It then integrates the field with RK4, one action after another. The next chunk starts from the last integrated action, not from scratch. This gives three benefits.

Smooth

Chunks join without jumps. The executed motion is one integral curve whose condition is refreshed every 8 steps.

Streaming

Each action is sent to the controller as soon as it is integrated. A whole chunk takes 5.7 ms with Euler on LIBERO.

Any control rate

The field lives in continuous time, so one model runs at a finer rate by integrating with a smaller step.

3Train by matching the path

$$\mathcal L_{\text{path}}^{\boldsymbol\delta}=\frac1H\sum_{k=1}^{H}\big\|\hat{\mathbf a}_{t+k}(\mathbf a_t+\boldsymbol\delta)-(\mathbf a_{t+k}+\rho^{k}\boldsymbol\delta)\big\|_2^2$$

Start from a demonstrated action, integrate $H$ steps, and match the whole path to the demonstration. The gradient flows back through the solver steps. No velocity labels are needed, so noisy demonstrations are not differentiated.

Contractive perturbation. Some segments start from a shifted point $\mathbf a_t+\boldsymbol\delta$, and the target shrinks the shift by $\rho^k$ at step $k$. The field thus learns to pull drift back to the demonstrations, at no cost at inference.

Path loss

Orange segments are the errors between the integrated steps and their targets. Training removes them.

After training

Paths from perturbed starts return to the demonstration.

4Optional diversity head

$$\dot{\mathbf a}(t')=\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t'),\,\mathbf s_t,\,\mathbf z_t\big)+\boldsymbol\lambda_k\odot\boldsymbol\epsilon$$

A deterministic field averages distinct demonstrated behaviors, e.g., passing an obstacle on the left or on the right, and heads into the obstacle. Since a mode is decided by the direction in which integration sets off, a small head reads the features of the field once per chunk and samples a direction $\boldsymbol\epsilon$ from a Gaussian mixture, with a rate $\boldsymbol\lambda_k$ per step. The offset tilts the field toward one route. The head is trained on detached inputs, so it leaves the gradient of $\mathbf f_\theta$ unchanged.

5The same field as an RL actor

In RL, the field acts at every control step ($H=1$) and the integrated action is the mean of the Gaussian actor. PPO or SAC trains the field through the solver steps with its own objective. Only the actor is replaced, and the critic and the algorithm stay unchanged.
