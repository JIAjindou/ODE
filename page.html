§ 01 — Idea

Actions should not jump either

A direct-action policy predicts each chunk of actions on its own, so the motion jumps where chunks meet. ODE policy learns how the action changes and integrates it over the robot's physical time, so each action follows from the previous one.

§ 03 — Method

Method

ODE policy replaces only the action head. The encoder, the data, and the training schedule stay the same.

1Learn a velocity, not an action

The images and the instruction are encoded into an embedding $\mathbf z_t$, as in any policy. The network $\mathbf f_\theta$ outputs how the action should change, given the action $\mathbf a(t)$ the robot is commanded to reach and its proprioception $\mathbf s_t$. Time $t$ is the physical time of the robot.

Because the field sees the current action, each step depends on where the motion has arrived. A direct-action policy outputs a whole chunk at once and cannot react to its own intermediate actions.

$$\dot{\mathbf a}(t)=\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t),\,\mathbf s_t,\,\mathbf z_t\big)$$

2Integrate chunk by chunk

Every $H=8$ steps, the policy reads the observation once and holds $\mathbf z_t$ and $\mathbf s_t$ fixed. It then integrates the field with RK4, one action after another, and the next chunk starts from the last integrated action. This gives three benefits.

$$\hat{\mathbf a}_{t+k+1}=\hat{\mathbf a}_{t+k}+\int_{t+k}^{t+k+1}\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t'),\mathbf s_t,\mathbf z_t\big)\,\mathrm dt'$$

Smooth

Chunks join without jumps. The motion is one integral curve whose condition is refreshed every 8 steps.

Streaming

Each action is sent as soon as it is integrated. A chunk takes 5.7 ms with Euler, against 61 ms for FM-DiT.

Any control rate

The field lives in continuous time, so one model runs at a finer rate by integrating with a smaller step.

3Train by matching the path

Start from a demonstrated action, integrate $H$ steps, and match the whole path. The gradient flows back through the solver, and no velocity labels are needed.

Contractive perturbation. Some segments start from a shifted point $\mathbf a_t+\boldsymbol\delta$, and the target shrinks the shift by $\rho^k$ at step $k$. The field learns to pull drift back, at no cost at inference.

$$\mathcal L_{\text{path}}^{\boldsymbol\delta}=\frac1H\sum_{k=1}^{H}\big\|\hat{\mathbf a}_{t+k}(\mathbf a_t+\boldsymbol\delta)-(\mathbf a_{t+k}+\rho^{k}\boldsymbol\delta)\big\|_2^2$$

During training

Orange segments are the errors between the integrated steps and their targets. Training removes them.

After training

Paths from perturbed starts return to the demonstration.

4Optional diversity head

A deterministic field averages distinct behaviors, e.g., passing an obstacle on the left or on the right, and heads into the obstacle. A small head reads the features of the field once per chunk and samples a direction $\boldsymbol\epsilon$ with a rate $\boldsymbol\lambda_k$ per step, which tilts the field toward one route. It is trained on detached inputs, so the gradient of $\mathbf f_\theta$ stays unchanged.

$$\dot{\mathbf a}(t')=\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t'),\mathbf s_t,\mathbf z_t\big)+\boldsymbol\lambda_k\odot\boldsymbol\epsilon$$

5The same field as an RL actor

In RL, the field acts at every control step ($H=1$), and the integrated action is the mean of the Gaussian actor. PPO or SAC trains the field through the solver steps with its own objective. Only the actor is replaced, and the critic and the algorithm stay unchanged.

$$\pi_\theta(\mathbf a_t\mid\cdot)=\mathcal N\big(\hat{\mathbf a}_t,\boldsymbol\sigma^2\big),\quad \hat{\mathbf a}_t=\int_0^1\mathbf f_\theta\big(\mathbf a(t'),\mathbf s_t,\mathbf z_t\big)\,\mathrm dt'$$
